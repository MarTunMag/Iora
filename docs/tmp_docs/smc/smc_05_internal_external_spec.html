Internal vs External Structure — Flint Implementation Spec

This section formalises how Flint replaces traditional Pine Script lookback-based swing detection with a zero-lag, zone-derived internal/external structure system. Every swing point is confirmed by a zone break event — no arbitrary parameters, no lag, no single-TF simulation. The spec maps directly to INTERNAL_EXTERNAL_STRUCTURE_SPEC.md at C:\Flint\docs\strategy\concepts\.

Core principle: In Flint, there are NO Pine Script lookback windows. Internal swings = current TF zone chain. External swings = parent TF zone boundaries. The full cascade: M1ext = M5int, M5ext = M15int, M15ext = H1int, H1ext = H4int, H4ext = Dint, Dext = Wint, Wext = MNint. External is confirmed at the CHoCH/BOS event — zero lag. Eight TFs, one rule, one mechanism.

Why Pine Script lookback detection fails

Traditional Pine Script indicators use detectSwings with ta.highest/ta.lowest lookbacks and checkMarketStructure for BOS/CHoCH classification. When you have real multi-TF zone data, every one of these mechanisms is unnecessary — and actively harmful. Four specific problems:

#Pine Script ProblemWhy It FailsFlint Solution
1 Arbitrary parameters ta.highest(length) / ta.lowest(length) require a user-chosen lookback length. Different lengths = different swings = different structure reads. The "correct" length depends on market conditions that the parameter itself cannot know. Zone fire events (HA color flip) define swings mechanically. No parameter to choose — the market state itself produces the swing point.
2 Inherent lag Lookback-based swing detection confirms a high/low only after length bars have passed without exceeding it. The confirmation arrives late — often after the next leg has already started. You miss the early entry window. Zone-based external confirmation happens at the CHoCH/BOS event — the moment price body-closes through the zone boundary. Zero lag beyond the candle close itself.
3 Single-TF simulation Pine's detectSwings operates on a single chart timeframe. To get "HTF structure," it either applies the same lookback to a higher TF (doubling the lag) or uses security() calls that introduce repainting risk. Neither produces genuine multi-TF structure. Flint receives real zone data from every TF simultaneously (ha_supply_demand_zones.pine with 8-TF support). Internal = current TF zones. External = parent TF zone boundaries. No simulation needed.
4 No zone awareness Pine's structure detection knows about swing highs and lows but has no concept of supply/demand zones. It cannot determine if a CHoCH is sourcing from an HTF zone (the validity filter from Part 1). It treats all structural breaks equally regardless of where they originate. Every BOS/CHoCH in Flint carries its zone context: which zone fired, which zone was broken, what the parent TF zone boundaries are. The HTF validity filter is built-in.

External swing confirmation via zone breaks

The key mechanical insight: you don't need a lookback to find a swing high or low. A swing is confirmed the moment price proves it by breaking through the zone that created it.

External high confirmation

1. Price rallies → supply zone fires at the peak (HA red flip) 2. Below the supply, a demand zone exists (the HL that launched the rally) 3. Price body-closes below the HH-making demand zone's bottom → The supply at the peak is NOW confirmed as the external top (Until this break, it could have been an internal swing — just a pause in continuation)

External low confirmation

1. Price drops → demand zone fires at the trough (HA blue flip) 2. Above the demand, a supply zone exists (the LH that launched the decline) 3. Price body-closes above the LL-making supply zone's top → The demand at the trough is NOW confirmed as the external bottom

The unbroken chain qualifier

Unbroken chain = zone chain count

An external swing is only valid if the zones forming it are part of an unbroken chain. The chain is: Dem LL → Sup LH → Dem LL → Sup LH → ... → Dem HH (or mirror for tops). A consumed/broken zone interrupts the sequence and resets the count. This maps directly to the existing H1 zone chain count in the Wave Navigator indicator.

Practically: if a demand zone in the middle of the sequence gets broken (price body-closes through it), the entire chain below that point is invalidated. The external swing identification restarts from the break point.

What "until this break, it could have been internal" means in practice

Look at the weekly external higher high at the right edge of the chart. Before price body-closed below the demand zone that launched the rally to that high, it could have been just a weekly internal swing — a pause before continuation to even higher levels. The moment the demand broke, the supply at the peak was confirmed as the weekly external top. Until that confirmation, it's Schrödinger's swing — simultaneously internal and external, resolved only by the break event.

This is why you can't use lookback windows to find external swings. A lookback would label the swing before the confirmation event — and sometimes be wrong. The zone-based approach waits for the confirmation (body close through zone) and is never wrong about the classification, only about the timing of when you know.

Full cascade propagation — how structure flows

The cascade has two reading directions. Top-down sets what you're allowed to trade. Bottom-up tells you when to enter. Both are always active simultaneously.

Top-down: context framing (MN → M1)

Bottom-up: early confirmation (M1 → MN)

M1 CHoCH fires → M5 sub-wave direction confirmed → M5 CHoCH fires → M15 zone leg complete (push exhausted or continuation confirmed) → M15 CHoCH fires → H1 sub-wave complete (H1 push TL break) → H1 CHoCH fires → creates H4 structure (H4 HH/HL/LH/LL confirmed EARLY) → H4 structural event → creates D structure (D HH/HL/LH/LL) → D structural event → creates W structure (W HH/HL/LH/LL) → W structural event → creates MN structure (extremely rare)
The timing asymmetry

An M1 CHoCH fires in ~1 minute. The H4 candle it belongs to won't close for hours. The D candle won't close until end of session. But the M1 CHoCH is already telling you the H4 and D events are mechanically inevitable. This is the entire edge: you know the HTF outcome before it's visible on the HTF chart.

Zone lifecycle through the cascade

A zone isn't static. As price cascades through timeframes, zones transition through a lifecycle. Understanding this lifecycle is critical because the same zone acts differently at each stage.

The four lifecycle states

1
Active Order Block (OB)

Zone fires on HA color flip. Unbroken. Acts as supply (sellers defend) or demand (buyers defend). This is the entry zone for Mode A retests. On this TF, it's an internal swing. On the child TF, it's the external boundary.

2
Consumed / Invalidated

Price body-closes through the zone. The zone is consumed — removed from the active supply/demand stack. This IS the BOS or CHoCH event. The zone no longer acts as order flow in its original direction. On the child TF, the external boundary has moved.

3
Breaker Block (polarity flipped)

After a full BOS through prior structure (HH then LL break, or vice versa), the consumed OB flips polarity. Old demand → new supply. Old supply → new demand. Price retests the breaker block and reacts in the opposite direction — trapped traders exit. This is the Mode C entry zone on pullback. On the parent TF, this breaker corresponds to the zone that preceded the structural flip.

4
Mitigation Block (failure swing)

When an LH or HL zone is broken (failure swing — no new HH/LL was made), it becomes a mitigation block. The traders who entered at the LH are trapped. When price returns, they exit at breakeven or small loss → the zone provides resistance/support on retest. Unlike breakers (which come from BOS through structure), mitigation blocks come from failed attempts.

How the lifecycle cascades across TFs

The key insight: a zone's lifecycle stage on one TF determines what it becomes on adjacent TFs.

Practical rule — how to use the target stack

When you enter a growth trade (Path A/B/E), your take-profit targets are the next unbroken OBs on the same or higher TF. Your pullback management uses the breaker/mitigation blocks on intermediate TFs. When price arrives at a target, look at the child TF for the reaction — M15/M5 CHoCH at the target zone tells you whether it holds or breaks.

The cascade always answers: "Where is price going?" (next OB on higher TF) and "Is it done?" (child TF CHoCH at the target).

Implementation architecture (StructureState)

The Flint implementation replaces Pine's detectSwings and checkMarketStructure with four components per timeframe:

ComponentReplacesPurpose
StructureState class (per TF) Pine's swing detection state Holds current internal swings (zone chain), external boundaries (from parent TF), zone count, trend direction, last BOS/CHoCH level. One instance per TF in the cascade.
get_structure_swings() Pine's detectSwings() Derives swing points from zone fire events. No lookback parameter. When a zone fires (HA color flip), the swing is registered. Classification (HH/LH/HL/LL) comes from comparing to the previous zone of the same type (Rule 1 from Part 3).
classify_break() Pine's checkMarketStructure() When a zone is broken (body close through), determines if the break is BOS or CHoCH using the zone classification shortcut: HH/LL zone breaks → BOS. LH/HL zone breaks → CHoCH. No tracing required.
check_cross_tf_propagation() No Pine equivalent After each zone break, checks if the break crosses the parent TF threshold (from the cross-TF propagation table in Part 3). If H1 supply top > prev H4 supply top → H4 HH confirmed. Triggers parent TF StructureState update.

Bar replay validation checklist

Phase 1 validation uses TradingView's bar replay mode to confirm the implementation matches real price action. Five specific tests, each verifiable visually:

1
Internal/external match

At each swing point identified by Flint, verify: is the label correct? Internal swings (dashed, no +) should be sub-moves within a larger leg. External swings (solid, + suffix) should be the major structural pivots that define the trend. If an internal swing is labelled external (or vice versa), the parent TF boundary inheritance is broken.

2
Parent TF boundary correspondence

At each external swing on TF N, verify that the corresponding parent TF (N+1) zone boundary is at the same price level. Example: H1 external high should correspond to the H4 internal swing high. If the levels diverge, the cascade propagation is miscalculating the cross-TF threshold.

3
Chain count accuracy

Count the unbroken zone chain manually on the chart. Compare to Flint's zone count display. The counts must match exactly. A mismatch means either (a) a consumed zone isn't being detected, or (b) the chain is not resetting on zone break. Check particularly at CHoCH events where the chain should reset.

4
Zero-lag confirmation

When a BOS/CHoCH fires in replay, the swing label should appear on the same candle as the body close through the zone. Not one bar later, not after a lookback confirmation period. If the label appears late, there is still latent lag in the detection logic — likely a ta.highest/ta.lowest remnant or an off-by-one in the bar indexing.

5
Cross-TF cascade timing

In replay: when M15 CHoCH fires, does the H1 zone update on the same H1 candle (or the next one)? When H1 CHoCH fires, does the H4 state update before the next H4 candle closes? The cascade should propagate upward within one parent-TF candle of the triggering event. A delay longer than one parent candle means the propagation check frequency is too low.

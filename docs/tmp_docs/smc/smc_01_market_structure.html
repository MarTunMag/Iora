Part 1

Market Structure Foundations

The three market states

Before BOS and CHoCH matter, classify the current regime. Every structural event is a transition between or within these three states — at every timeframe simultaneously.

Uptrend
Higher Highs (HH) + Higher Lows (HL). Buyers expanding each swing. Only look for longs. Short into HH supply zones only to scalp.
Downtrend
Lower Highs (LH) + Lower Lows (LL). Sellers expanding each swing. Only look for shorts. Buy into LL demand zones only to scalp or position-reverse.
Consolidation
Price moves sideways — no expanding HH/HL and no descending LH/LL. Liquidity building. No directional entries until a BOS resolves the range in one direction.
The institutional cycle — Accumulation → Manipulation → Distribution (AMD)
Every structural move follows this three-phase cycle. Accumulation: institutions build positions inside a consolidation range — sideways price action, tight candles, low volume. Manipulation: price spikes beyond the range boundary to sweep stop-losses and trigger breakout entries from retail traders (the "liquidity grab" or "stop hunt"). Distribution: with retail trapped, price reverses sharply in the intended direction — this is the displacement move that creates the BOS and the new supply/demand zone. In Flint, zone formation events (HA color flips) occurring after a manipulation spike are higher-quality zones than those forming from gradual drift.

How supply and demand zones form

A zone is not just any price area — it forms when price leaves a consolidation area with an impulsive, displacement move (large-body candle, little to no overlap with prior candles). The consolidation candles before the impulse are the zone. In Flint's Heikin-Ashi system, this maps to the HA color flip: the last candle(s) of the old color before the new-color impulse = the zone body. Zones formed from weak, grinding moves carry less institutional conviction and are more likely to fail on retest.

External vs internal structure

Every timeframe contains two simultaneous layers of swing points. Collapsing them into one is the single most common cause of false CHoCH signals and entries against the trend.

  • External highs/lows: Major structural pivots that define the trend on that TF. Institutions place orders here. Only these count for BOS and CHoCH detection.
  • Internal highs/lows: Sub-swings inside each external leg. On H1 these are M15/M5 moves. Retail traders read these as reversals — institutions use them as liquidity pools to sweep before continuing.

iBOS / iCHoCH vs eBoS / eCHoCH

iBoS = internal break at sub-swing level. Move is retracing and continuing. eBoS = external break at major structural pivot. This is what counts. iCHoCH = sub-level warning. eCHoCH = the actual structural flip and the only one that changes your bias.

eBoS (green): The two external structural breaks — define the trend and are the reference points for CHoCH detection.
eCHoCH (red): The one valid character change — breaks the external HL that preceded the last eBoS.
iBoS / iCHoCH: Sub-wave events inside larger legs. Use for LTF entry timing only.

iBOS vs eBoS — the filter that saves you from fake signals

When you see a swing break on your chart, the first question is always: is this breaking an external structural pivot, or is it a sub-swing inside the current leg? An iBOS/iCHoCH on H1 confirms nothing at H1 — it is M15/M5 noise. A valid H1 eBOS requires an H1 candle body close through an H1 external swing.

Break of Structure (BOS) — trend continuation

Bullish BOS
Body closes above previous external HH. Buyers extend the swing. Chain count +1. Stay long, enter on demand pullbacks.
Bearish BOS
Body closes below previous external LL. Sellers extend the swing. Chain count +1. Stay short, enter on supply retests.
Hard rules — no exceptions
  • Wicks never count. A wick through a level is a liquidity sweep — institutions triggering stop-losses above/below equal highs/lows. Only a candle body close creates a BOS.
  • Timeframe-specific close. H1 BOS = H1 body close. D1 BOS = D1 body close.
  • External swings only. Breaking an internal sub-swing is an iBOS and does not increment the structural chain count.
  • Displacement validates quality. A body close that barely clears the level (1–2 pips) on a small-body candle is a weak BOS — valid structurally but low-confidence for entries. A strong BOS shows displacement: large-body candle closing decisively past the level. In Flint, flag BOS events where the close distance from the broken level is less than the ATR(14) × 0.3 as "weak BOS" for reduced entry weighting.

Change of Character (CHoCH) — potential reversal signal

A CHoCH is a warning — not a confirmation. The BOS that follows the CHoCH in the new direction is the confirmation. Never enter on CHoCH alone without higher-timeframe context alignment.

Liquidity — the fuel behind every structural event

Every BOS, CHoCH, and zone formation is driven by liquidity — clusters of resting orders (stop-losses, pending entries) that institutions target to fill their large positions. Understanding where liquidity pools sit explains why price sweeps a level before reversing.

Liquidity types (for Flint implementation)

Buy-side liquidity (BSL) — resting above equal highs, swing highs, and HH/LH supply zone tops. These are buy stop-losses from existing short positions and buy-stop entries from breakout traders. When price sweeps BSL, institutions are selling into the wave of buy orders — the sweep is the setup for the bearish reversal. In Flint, detect BSL as areas where 2+ highs cluster within ATR(14) × 0.1 of each other.

Sell-side liquidity (SSL) — resting below equal lows, swing lows, and LL/HL demand zone bottoms. These are sell stop-losses from existing long positions and sell-stop entries from breakdown traders. When price sweeps SSL, institutions are buying into the wave of sell orders — the sweep is the setup for the bullish reversal. In Flint, detect SSL as areas where 2+ lows cluster within ATR(14) × 0.1 of each other.

The AMD pattern — Accumulation (range/consolidation) → Manipulation (liquidity sweep beyond the range) → Distribution (displacement move in the true direction). This three-phase cycle repeats at every timeframe. A valid BOS following a liquidity sweep is the highest-quality signal — it confirms institutional accumulation has completed and distribution is underway.

HTF zone validity — the rule that filters everything

"The CHoCH must come from a higher timeframe supply/demand or else it is invalid." This is the most important filter in the system. A CHoCH on H1 firing at an H4 zone is high-probability. A CHoCH firing in open space between zones is noise.

The "last BOS" rule and zone classification shortcut

In any downtrend there are multiple LH candidates. Only one is the valid CHoCH level: the LH that directly preceded the most recent bearish BOS. Earlier LHs are consumed structure.

Zone classification shortcut — no tracing required:
HH or LL zone breaks → BOS (structural, trend continues, chain +1)
LH or HL zone breaks → CHoCH (corrective, reversal signal)

A LH zone is by definition corrective — supply top below previous. Breaking it is always a character change. A HH zone is structural — breaking it extends the trend. The zone classification IS the answer.

Zone Classification System

One rule, every timeframe, every zone. Master this and BOS/CHoCH detection becomes a simple comparison — not a subjective judgment.

The one rule — HH / LH / HL / LL

When a new zone fires (HA color flip), compare it to the previous zone of the same type on the same TF:

Supply zone (blue → red flip)
New top > prev supply top → HH (structural)
New top ≤ prev supply top → LH (corrective)
Demand zone (red → blue flip)
New bot < prev demand bot → LL (structural)
New bot ≥ prev demand bot → HL (corrective)

BOS/CHoCH as mechanical zone-break outcomes

When a zone is broken (body close through it):
HH or LL zone breaks → BOS — structural, trend confirmed, chain +1
LH or HL zone breaks → CHoCH — corrective, reversal signal

No tracing required. The zone classification IS the BOS/CHoCH answer.

Zone nesting — child inside parent signals

A child zone forming inside a parent zone is a signal. The direction match determines whether it confirms continuation or flags a terminal reversal.

Containment check

A child zone is nested if and only if: child.top ≤ parent.top AND child.bot ≥ parent.bot. Both edges must be inside. Partial overlap does not count.

Opposing nesting (most important): An H1 zone opposing the H4 zone it sits inside = WILL be broken. The H4 zone represents the larger force.

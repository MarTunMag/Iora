Full mechanical walkthrough — step by step

Reading the SMC Mechanical Entry Models chart left-to-right as a sequential process. Each step corresponds to a visible section of the chart and a specific mechanical rule.

1
D1 macro classification — establish the boundary

Before anything else: identify the D1 supply and demand zones. The grey band at the top of the chart is the D1/HTF supply. The grey band at the bottom is the D1/HTF demand. All subsequent analysis operates between these two boundaries. A CHoCH sourcing from the D1 supply = valid bearish flip. A CHoCH from the D1 demand = valid bullish flip. Anything in between = lower probability until the next boundary is approached.

2
Bullish impulse from D1 demand — H4/H1 BOS chain

Price bounces from the D1 demand zone. At H4: first HL fires (H4 HL), then H4 HH (supply top exceeds prev H4 supply top — cross-TF threshold crossed, D HL confirmed). At H1: individual H1 demand zones fire on each pullback (HL classification). Each H1 BOS = chain count +1. Context mode: FLIP transitioning to RIDE (bullish). Entry: Mode A — short retests of H1 supply zones on each LH during the correction phase, or Mode B after D HH confirmed.

3
Structural peak — entry at H4/D supply confluence

Price approaches the D1 supply zone. H4 supply fires with top below previous D supply top → H4 HH registers as D LH. This is the earliest confirmation that the D bearish impulse is intact and the rally was corrective. At this point: if H4 zone count shows 5+ H1 supply zones in the correction sequence, or if the boundary zone is breached, this is the CHoCH trigger for the bearish phase to resume. Context mode transitions: RIDE (bearish). The annotation boxes on the chart at this zone describe the exact zone conditions and entry type.

4
First bearish LH — Mode A entry setup

After the CHoCH fires at the D1 supply, the first H1 LH supply zone appears. This is the Mode A entry zone: pre-D LH confirmation, H1 zone count 1 (first zone of the new bearish leg). Entry: short at H1 supply zone top (or bottom for aggressive). SL: above the H1 supply zone top + spread_buf. The M15 CHoCH inside the H1 supply zone (M15 LH) is the confirmation signal — it means the H1 supply zone is being defended. M1 CHoCH inside the last M5 demand = Path E earliest entry.

5
BOS chain extending — zone count increments

Each H1 BOS (close below previous H1 LL) = chain count +1. Each count step has a zone classification: LH (if H1 supply top lower than previous = corrective zone in the push) or HH (if higher = structural extension). Mode B activates after D LH is confirmed: aggressive entries at M5/M15 supply zones on each LH, not waiting for H1 level retests. The push TL (connecting LH supply tops) acts as a dynamic resistance guide.

6
Zone 6 — boundary (last normal entry)

When H1 zone count reaches 6, the current zone is the boundary zone. The boundary spans from the last unbroken H1 supply top to the structural LL low. This is the last valid Mode A or Mode B entry point. After zone 6, the next zone (7) is corrective — holds only, no new entries against the main trend. A close above the boundary supply top simultaneously: (1) breaks the boundary zone AND (2) confirms H4 HH. Implement as single boundary event in Flint rule engine.

7
Zones 7-8 — correction phase (terminal approach)

H1 zones 7 and 8 are the correction sub-waves. Zone 7 = first corrective HL (demand zone bot above previous demand bot). Zone 8 = second HL — correction Wave B. At this point: hold existing positions, no new Mode A/B entries. Watch for: M15 TL break (M15 bearish TL connecting M15 LH tops breaks upward = H1 demand about to form), M1 HL (Path E earliest reversal signal), H4 demand zone appearing below the broken D LL (H4 counter-zone = terminal signal in Spring Leaf).

8
Terminal exhaustion — reversal setup

5+ H1 supply zones complete (or 5+3 if correction present) + H4 counter-zone (H4 demand below broken D LL) = terminal exhaustion. Context mode transitions to FLIP. Sequence: H1 HL fires (first CHoCH at H1 = correction starting) → H1 HH fires (BOS = reversal confirmed at H1) → M15 HL inside H1 demand (nested bullish) → M5 HL (bullish building) → M1 HL (CHoCH) inside last M5 supply = Path E entry. Or wait for boundary break which simultaneously = H4 HH confirmation = full FLIP mode trigger.

9
X — invalidated zone

The X mark on the chart (visible in the right section) = a zone that has been consumed/invalidated. A zone is consumed when price closes through it (body close beyond the zone boundary). Once consumed, the zone no longer acts as supply or demand — it has either become a breaker block (if the close was a structural BOS) or is simply eliminated from the active zone list. In Flint's zone tracking: consumed zones are removed from the active supply/demand stack. The X provides visual confirmation of zone consumption on the chart.

How TLs bridge internal → external structure

The key connection: Internal structure is the child TF's zone sequence. Trendlines drawn on internal swing points tell you precisely when the internal move ends. When the internal TL breaks, the internal push/pullback is done — and at that moment, the parent TF's external structure has shifted (eBOS or eCHoCH). This is the timing bridge between the two layers.

Recall the cascade identity from Part 7: Dint = H4ext, H4int = H1ext, H1int = M15ext, M15int = M5ext. Internal swings on the parent TF are literally the external structure of the child TF. So when we draw a trendline connecting the internal swing points on the parent, we're drawing it on the external structure of the child — and the child's TL break is the signal that tells us the parent's internal move is done.

The full sequence: TL break → breaker zone → reversal entry

This is where trendlines, internal/external structure, and breaker zones all connect into one mechanical sequence:

1
Internal push builds external structure

The child TF's zones make a sequence of LH supply tops (descending) or HL demand bots (ascending) — this IS the parent's internal push. Draw the impulse TL connecting these child external swing points.

2
Internal impulse TL breaks

When the child TF's impulse TL breaks, the internal push on the parent is over. This is the moment the parent's eBOS or eCHoCH fires. The last zone before the break = the zone that failed to hold = the breaker zone.

3
Correction builds (internal pullback)

After the impulse TL breaks, the child structure shifts — now you see a correction (HL bots ascending or LH tops descending). Draw the correction TL on these new internal swing points. This correction = the parent's internal pullback toward the breaker zone.

4
Correction TL breaks → trend resumes

When the correction TL breaks, the pullback is done — the original trend direction resumes. Price pushes back toward the breaker zone from Step 2. The child-of-child TF's TL break confirms the timing of this reversal.

5
Entry at the breaker zone

Price reaches the breaker zone. Use the grandchild TF's CHoCH (M1 CHoCH inside the zone) as your precision entry trigger. The TL break cascade from parent → child → grandchild gives you the full "when" — the breaker zone gives you the "where."

Worked example: bearish D push building, then reversal

D internal push down (bearish): H4 external makes LH → LH → LH (supply tops descending) → Draw H4 impulse TL connecting these LH supply tops → Inside each H4 leg: H1 zones push price to each new H4 LL   H4 impulse TL breaks UP: → D internal push is OVER — D eCHoCH fires → The last H4 LH supply zone that held before the break = BREAKER ZONE → This H4 supply zone flips polarity — it was resistance, now it's support for re-entry   H4 correction builds (pullback): → H4 now makes HL → HL (demand bots ascending = correction TL) → H1 internal TL on this correction: H1 LH tops descending inside the pullback   H4 correction TL breaks DOWN: → The pullback is done — bearish push RESUMES toward new D LL → Confirmed early by: H1 impulse TL break (H1 LL fires = H4 LL)   → ENTRY: short at the H4 breaker zone on retest, with M1 CHoCH timing
Why this matters: Without TLs, you see zones appearing and breaking but have no sense of when the internal push ends or when the pullback completes. The TL on internal structure gives you the timing. The internal/external cascade gives you the which level. The breaker zone gives you the where. Together, they form a complete mechanical system: you know when the internal move is done (TL break), what external structure that creates (eBOS/eCHoCH), where the re-entry zone is (breaker), and how to time the entry (child TF's TL break + M1 CHoCH).

Practical rule

Impulse TL break = potential reversal starting. The push that built the trend is broken — look for the breaker zone that formed.
Correction TL break = trend resumes. The pullback failed — price pushes back toward the breaker zone. This is your entry timing.
The M15 TL break is your primary entry gate — it confirms the H1 sub-wave is complete and is required for Path A (terminal reversal), Path B (stair-step), and early confirmation cascade entries.
